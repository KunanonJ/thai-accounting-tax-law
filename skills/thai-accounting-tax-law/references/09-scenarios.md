Step-by-step walkthroughs of situations small businesses meet every month. Each one says what to do, which form to file and what to check. The numbers are examples; replace them with yours.

## Scenario 1: Hiring a Thai freelancer (individual)

**Situation:** your company pays a freelance designer (an individual, not VAT-registered) 25,000 THB for a logo.

**Steps**

1. **Check the payee type.** An individual → form **ภ.ง.ด.3**. A company would be ภ.ง.ด.53.
2. **Work out the WHT** on the pre-VAT amount. No VAT here.

   | Line | Normal transfer | Through e-WHT |
   |---|---|---|
   | Fee | 25,000.00 | 25,000.00 |
   | WHT | 3% = 750.00 [V] | 1% = 250.00 [V] |
   | **Pay the freelancer** | **24,250.00** | **24,750.00** |

3. **Pay** the net amount.
4. **Give the freelancer a 50 ทวิ certificate** for the 750 THB withheld [S]. With e-WHT, the bank issues electronic evidence instead [S PEAK].
5. **File ภ.ง.ด.3 and remit 750 THB** by the 7th of next month on paper, or the 15th online [V].
6. **Stamp duty:** a hire-of-work contract carries 1 THB per 1,000 THB, here 25 THB. The contractor (ผู้รับจ้าง) is responsible for stamping it [S RD regional article].
7. **Record it** as an expense with the invoice and transfer slip attached.

**Check:** the 1,000 THB rule. Payments of 1,000 THB or more need withholding [S FlowAccount].

## Scenario 2: Paying office rent

**Situation A:** you rent an office from an **individual** landlord for 30,000 THB a month on a 12-month lease.

1. WHT on rent is **5%** [V]: 30,000 × 5% = **1,500**. Pay the landlord **28,500**.
2. File **ภ.ง.ด.3** monthly by the 7th (paper) or 15th (online) [V]; give the landlord a 50 ทวิ.
3. Stamp duty on the lease is 0.1% of total rent: 360,000 × 0.1% = **360 THB** [S FlowAccount]. Who pays depends on the lease terms and the stamp-duty schedule [U]; agree it in the contract.

**Situation B:** the landlord is a **VAT-registered company**.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Rent | 30,000.00 |
| VAT 7% | 2,100.00 |
| Invoice total | 32,100.00 |
| WHT 5% on pre-VAT rent | 1,500.00 |
| **You transfer** | **30,600.00** |

File **ภ.ง.ด.53** instead of ภ.ง.ด.3. If you are VAT-registered, claim the 2,100 as input VAT with the tax invoice [S].

## Scenario 3: Buying software from abroad

**Situation:** your company pays 20 USD a month to a US software company.

1. **Look at the invoice.** Does it show **Thai VAT 7%** and a Thai VAT number? Large foreign e-service providers registered in Thailand charge it to non-VAT buyers [S FlowAccount].
   - **Yes, and you are not VAT-registered:** nothing extra to file for VAT; the VAT is part of the cost.
   - **Yes, and you are VAT-registered:** the provider usually shouldn't charge you Thai VAT once you give your VAT number; ask your accountant how to handle it [U].
   - **No Thai VAT on it:** a VAT-registered buyer self-assesses VAT on **ภ.พ.36** and claims it back [S]. Whether a non-registered buyer must file ภ.พ.36 is **[U]**.
2. **Withholding tax (ภ.ง.ด.54):** payments abroad can carry 15% WHT, possibly reduced by a tax treaty [V PwC]. Whether a SaaS subscription counts as a royalty or a service, and whether you must withhold, is **[U]**. Ask your accountant before you start or stop.
3. **Record it in baht.** Use the baht amount on your card or bank statement, and keep the receipt. If you have both a statement amount and an exchange rate, agree with your accountant which one to use and stick to it.

## Scenario 4: Crossing the VAT threshold

**Situation:** sales are about 160,000 THB a month, and you are not VAT-registered.

| Month | Cumulative sales | Above 1.8M? |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1,600,000 | No |
| 11 | 1,760,000 | No |
| 12 | 1,920,000 | **Yes** |

1. You must register once turnover **exceeds 1.8M THB in a year**; apply (ภ.พ.01) within **30 days** [S FlowAccount].
2. Plan ahead: decide whether your prices will include VAT (a 107 THB price becomes 100 + 7 VAT) or add it on top, and update your invoices and contracts.
3. After registration: issue tax invoices, charge 7%, and file **ภ.พ.30** every month, even with no sales [S].
4. Registering late means back-VAT from the day you crossed the threshold, a penalty of up to 2× the tax and a 1.5% per month surcharge [S FlowAccount].

## Scenario 5: Hiring your first employee

**Situation:** you hire an employee on 30,000 THB a month.

1. **Social security:** 5% each from employee and employer, on wages up to 17,500 THB [S FlowAccount]: 17,500 × 5% = **875 THB each** per month. Remit by the **15th** of the following month [S FlowAccount].
2. **Withholding on salary:** deduct monthly WHT based on the employee's estimated annual personal income tax; payroll software does this calculation [S FlowAccount]. File **ภ.ง.ด.1** by the 7th (paper) or 15th (online).
3. **Year end:** file **ภ.ง.ด.1ก** by the end of February and give each employee a 50 ทวิ for the year [S FlowAccount].
4. A new juristic person is registered as an employer with the SSO automatically [S FlowAccount].

## Scenario 6: Receiving payment from a foreign customer

**Situation:** a foreign company pays your Thai company a commission or service fee by bank transfer.

1. **Issue your document** (invoice/receipt). Some platforms issue a self-billed invoice for you; keep it as your evidence.
2. **No Thai WHT** is deducted, because the payer is not a Thai taxpayer [S].
3. **Record it** as revenue in baht at the amount received, with the invoice and the bank's payment advice attached.
4. **VAT:** if you are VAT-registered, whether the service is zero-rated as an export of services depends on where it is performed and used; this is **[U]** for your case, so ask your accountant. If you are not VAT-registered, ask whether this income counts towards the 1.8M THB registration threshold [U].

## Scenario 7: Month-end close

A practical checklist to run in the first week of every month:

1. ☐ Every sale and expense from last month is recorded, with its document attached.
2. ☐ Each bank account is reconciled to the statement; match only at a difference of zero.
3. ☐ WHT for last month filed and paid: ภ.ง.ด.1 / 3 / 53 / 54 (by the 7th, or 15th online).
4. ☐ If VAT-registered: ภ.พ.30 (by the 15th, or 23rd online); ภ.พ.36 if you bought foreign services.
5. ☐ Social security remitted (by the 15th).
6. ☐ 50 ทวิ certificates sent to payees.
7. ☐ AR and AP ageing reviewed: who owes you, whom you owe.
8. ☐ Period locked in the accounting software once everything is filed.

See [chapter 8](08-flowaccount-howto.md) for doing these in FlowAccount.

## Scenario 8: Year-end for a limited company (31 December)

| When | Task |
|---|---|
| January | Close December; count stock and fixed assets; send the books to your auditor |
| By end of February | ภ.ง.ด.1ก if you have employees [S] |
| By 30 April | AGM approves the audited financial statements [S InfoQuest] |
| Within 14 days of the AGM | บอจ.5 shareholder list to DBD [S InfoQuest] |
| Within 1 month of the AGM | Financial statements to DBD e-Filing [S InfoQuest; S Matichon] |
| By 30 May | ภ.ง.ด.50 and pay the tax [V] |
| By 31 August | ภ.ง.ด.51 for the first half of the new year [V] |

A partnership skips the AGM and files its financial statements with DBD within 5 months of year end [S InfoQuest].

## Scenario 9: Converting a หจก. into a บจก.

See the full checklist in [chapter 1](01-business-entities.md). The key points:

1. The company takes over everything; the partnership is not liquidated [V CCC].
2. RD rulings say the transfer is not a sale, so no CIT, WHT, SBT or stamp duty on it [V].
3. Whether a stub-period ภ.ง.ด.50 for the partnership is needed is **[U]**; ask your accountant.
4. Switch names on documents by **document date**: before the conversion date the partnership, from that date the company.
5. A company must use a CPA auditor from its first year.
